When the destination rank specified in MPI_Send fails to match the expected source rank in MPI_Recv, the receiver cannot locate a matching message. A standard, blocking MPI_Recv will wait indefinitely under these conditions, causing the program to hang in a deadlock. To prevent this, the demo utilized a non-blocking MPI_Irecv combined with MPI_Test and a 3-second timeout, allowing the application to detect and report the mismatch rather than freezing.
Meanwhile, the sender remains unaffected in this scenario. Because the message was small (a single integer), MPI_Send completed normally despite the fact that the message was never actually received. However, this behavior relies entirely on the MPI implementation's ability to buffer small messages and cannot be guaranteed for larger payloads.
In mode 2, MPICH successfully completed the execution loop but aborted during MPI_Finalize, reporting one unmatched message because the data sent to rank 1 was never picked up. Conversely, if the destination rank is completely non-existent (such as mode 3 targeting rank 5 in a 2-process job), MPI_Send fails immediately. The runtime triggers an "Invalid rank" error, and the entire job terminates abruptly.
